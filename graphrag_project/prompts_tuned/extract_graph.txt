
-Goal-
Given a text document that is potentially relevant to this activity and a list of entity types, identify all entities of those types from the text and all relationships among the identified entities.

-Steps-
1. Identify all entities. For each identified entity, extract the following information:
- entity_name: Name of the entity, capitalized
- entity_type: One of the following types: [person, organization, customer organization, professional role, software product, software component, source-code repository, pull request, code review, issue, release, requirement, architecture decision record, technical design document, communication channel, meeting, test artifact, external integration, regulation, business process, expense, receipt image, financial record, approval policy, user-access role]
- entity_description: Comprehensive description of the entity's attributes and activities
Format each entity as ("entity"<|><entity_name><|><entity_type><|><entity_description>)

2. From the entities identified in step 1, identify all pairs of (source_entity, target_entity) that are *clearly related* to each other.
For each pair of related entities, extract the following information:
- source_entity: name of the source entity, as identified in step 1
- target_entity: name of the target entity, as identified in step 1
- relationship_description: explanation as to why you think the source entity and the target entity are related to each other
- relationship_strength: an integer score between 1 to 10, indicating strength of the relationship between the source entity and target entity
Format each relationship as ("relationship"<|><source_entity><|><target_entity><|><relationship_description><|><relationship_strength>)

3. Return output in English as a single list of all the entities and relationships identified in steps 1 and 2. Use **##** as the list delimiter.

4. If you have to translate into English, just translate the descriptions, nothing else!

5. When finished, output <|COMPLETE|>.

## Error handling

If the image cannot be read at all (too dark, too small, not a receipt), the reader returns an empty result with a
warning, and the app asks the employee to take a new photo or to fill in the fields by hand. The reader never blocks the
creation of an expense: in the worst case the employee types the fields, exactly as before Kvitta.

If the text recognition engine fails or times out, the expense API retries once and then creates the draft without
extracted fields. Such failures are counted and alerted on, since a sudden increase usually means a problem with the
engine rather than with the receipts.

## Performance

Reading a receipt takes about 1.2 seconds on the current API servers, of which almost one second is text recognition.
The app shows the draft as soon as the result is back, so the employee sees the fields filled in about two seconds after
taking the photo. The reader is stateless and can run on several API instances at once.

## Testing

The reader is tested at three levels:

- Unit tests per step, with small hand-made images (tests/receipts/test_reader.py, and tests/receipts/test_vat.py for
  the VAT rules).
- A regression set of fifty receipts with known correct fields, run on every pull request. A change that makes any of
  them worse fails the build. Twelve restaurant and hotel receipts were added to the set for the new VAT rates.
- A quality run on five hundred real receipts from the pilot, anonymised, run before each release. The target is at
  least 90 % fully correct.

## Worked examples

### A lunch receipt from a café

A typical receipt from a café near the office: the merchant name in large letters at the top, the address and
organisation number below, three line items, then SUMMA 142,00 and MOMS 25% 28,40. Step 3 groups SUMMA and 142,00 into
one line and the VAT line with its rate and amount into another. The amount becomes 142.00, the VAT 28.40 at 25 %, and
the check against the total passes. The date is printed twice, at the top as 2026-02-04 and at the bottom in the card
slip with a time; the top one wins. All four fields get a confidence above 0.9 and nothing is marked for review.

### A taxi receipt

Taxi receipts are narrow and often printed on thermal paper that fades within weeks. The pilot had many of them from
late January that were already pale. After the contrast step in image preparation the amount and date read reliably,
but the merchant line is often a company name followed by a car number, for example TAXI STOCKHOLM BIL 4711. The
merchant cleanup removes the car number, and the organisation number printed at the bottom identifies the company even
when the name is abbreviated. Taxi rides have 6 % VAT, which the reader now recognises.

### A restaurant dinner

A dinner receipt from a restaurant prints MOMS 12% on the food and MOMS 25% on the wine. The reader returns two VAT
lines, one at 12 % and one at 25 %, and checks that they add up to the total within one krona. Before KV-4 this receipt
was marked for review with the warning unsupported VAT rate, about one receipt in eight in the pilot; with the new VAT
rules nearly all restaurant receipts are read correctly. If the dinner was with a client, the employee marks it as
representation in the app, and only the deductible part of the VAT is exported.

### A hotel invoice as PDF

Hotels usually send a PDF invoice by email instead of a paper receipt. The PDF is rendered to an image and read like a
photo, but the text is sharp, so recognition scores are high. The date that matters is the check-out date, which hotels
print near the top, while the invoice date at the bottom can be days later. The rule that the topmost date wins gives
the right answer for all hotel invoices in the pilot. A room at 12 % and a minibar at 25 % give two VAT lines.

## Monitoring after release

Three numbers are tracked per week once the reader runs for all customers:

- The share of receipts where the employee changed at least one field. The pilot baseline is 11 %. A rise of more than
  two percentage points in a week is investigated.
- The share of fields marked for review. If it rises while the change rate stays the same, the confidence threshold is
  probably too cautious.
- The number of reader failures (empty results). These are alerted on directly, since they usually mean the text
  recognition engine is broken rather than the receipts being bad.

The numbers are computed from the stored reader results and the change log, so no extra data has to be collected. Any
change to the reader is compared against these numbers for the week before and the week after it is deployed.
